
# 🎯 QR Code Phishing (Quishing)

## 📌 Overview
QR Code Phishing, also known as **Quishing**, is a type of cyber attack where attackers use malicious QR codes to trick users into visiting fake websites and stealing sensitive information such as usernames, passwords, banking details, and personal data.

This project contains educational notes about QR Code Phishing, its working process, types, objectives, and prevention methods.

## 🔍 What is QR Code Phishing?
QR Code Phishing is a phishing technique that uses fake or malicious QR codes to redirect victims to fraudulent websites.

When users scan these QR codes, they may unknowingly expose their personal or financial information to attackers.

## ⚙️ How Does It Work?
1. Attackers create a malicious QR code.
2. They distribute it through emails, posters, messages, or social media.
3. Victims scan the QR code using their smartphones.
4. The QR code redirects them to a fake website.
5. Victims enter sensitive information.
6. Attackers collect the submitted information.

## 🧩 Types of QR Code Phishing
- **Email Quishing:** Malicious QR codes sent through emails.
- **Payment Quishing:** Fake QR codes used to steal payment information.
- **Website Quishing:** QR codes redirecting users to fake login pages.
- **Poster Quishing:** Malicious QR codes placed on public posters.
- **Social Media Quishing:** Fake QR codes shared on social media.

## 🎯 Objectives of Attackers
- Steal usernames and passwords.
- Collect banking information.
- Access personal accounts.
- Steal sensitive information.
- Distribute malware.
- Gain unauthorized access to systems.

## 🚨 Common Warning Signs
- Unknown or suspicious QR codes.
- Unexpected payment requests.
- Fake or unusual website URLs.
- Requests for passwords or OTPs.
- Suspicious login or verification pages.

## 🛡️ Prevention and Protection
1. Scan QR codes only from trusted sources.
2. Always check the URL before opening it.
3. Never share passwords or OTPs.
4. Avoid scanning unknown QR codes.
5. Use mobile security software.
6. Keep devices and applications updated.
7. Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).

## 📖 Real-World Example
A user receives an email claiming that their bank account requires verification. The email contains a QR code.

After scanning the QR code, the user is redirected to a fake banking website. When the user enters their login credentials, the attacker can steal that information.

## 📚 Key Takeaways
- QR Code Phishing is also called Quishing.
- It uses malicious QR codes to deceive users.
- It can target personal and financial information.
- Checking URLs before opening them is essential.
- User awareness is one of the best defenses.
